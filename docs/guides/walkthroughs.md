# Walkthroughs

Turn stills into a click-through walkthrough: each step a picture with a hotspot the viewer clicks, a tooltip or a card, and a chapter; a builder makes one, a player presents it.

A walkthrough is an ordered list of **steps**. Each step is a picture (a still from **Captures**, annotated or not), an optional **hotspot** (a rectangle on the picture the viewer clicks to go on), a **text** shown as a **tooltip** beside the hotspot or as a centred **modal** card, an optional **instruction** - what the learner does at the step, written as it would be spoken ("drag the Text file input onto the canvas") - and an optional **chapter** name that starts a new chapter at that step. A tooltip step with no hotspot has nothing to sit beside, so it is shown as a modal card. Read in order, the instructions are the walkthrough's **transcript**: the narration script of the narrated video to come.

Walkthroughs work in the **browser edition** as in the desktop app: the list, the builder and the player touch no screen. Only **taking a still** needs the desktop app ([Screen capture](capture.md)). A still can come from the **Capture** tile (annotate it if you like, then add it as a step) or, for a walkthrough made from a course workshop, from **Capture steps…** in the builder, which takes one still per step as you perform them.

A walkthrough can also be **imported from a course workshop** - or from several workshops of a course, a whole section or the whole course, with the chapters you do not want left out: each workshop's sections become the chapters, its numbered steps the steps, each with its instruction and the guide's screenshot where the guide has one; the steps the guide shows no picture for are captured afterwards in the desktop app as you perform them - every click a step, or one key press per step. **Export to course…** then writes the captured steps back into each step's own workshop guide, each as a figure after its step - the content manager's annotated image, which marks the hotspot with a numbered pin once the Content Manager draws that form (it does not yet: see *Export to course*) - and can put the interactive player beside the guide. See *Import a workshop*, *Capture the steps* and *Export to course* below.

## What you see

The Dashboard's **Walkthroughs** tile opens the list. The sidebar has no entry for it - it lists Dashboard, Projects, Docs and Settings - and lights **Dashboard** while you are on the list or in the builder (the player fills the window, without the sidebar). The list's address, `/walkthroughs`, works as a bookmark.

The **list** shows a card per walkthrough: the first step's picture (the first step that has one), the name, "12 steps · 3 chapters", when it was last saved and whose it is, and **Open** (the builder), **Play** (greyed out until the walkthrough has a step), **Duplicate** (the copy is named "<name> (copy)", and the page says so) and a bin, **Delete walkthrough**, which asks first ("Delete *name* and its 12 steps? The stills it was made from are kept.", with **Delete**). **New walkthrough** asks for a name and opens the builder; **Import workshop…** makes one from a course's workshops - one, a section, or the whole course (below). When there is none yet, the page says how one is made.

The **builder** has three columns under a toolbar. The toolbar holds the walkthrough's **Name**, **Undo**, **Redo**, **Save**, **Play**, **Transcript**, **Export to course…**, in the desktop app **Capture steps…**, and a status line that reads **Saved** when the walkthrough is as it was last saved or loaded, **Unsaved changes** whenever it differs, and a one-off line ("Nothing to save.", "Reloaded", "Imported …", or a refusal such as "Could not save: …") until the next change; **Saving…** while a save runs. On the left, the **step list**: a thumbnail and a number for each step (a dashed **No picture** tile for a step that has none yet), the chapter name when the step starts one, arrows to move a step up or down, a bin to delete it (asked first), and **Add step…** at the foot. In the middle, the selected step's picture with its hotspot - or, for a step without a picture, a placeholder: "No picture yet - Capture it in the desktop app (Capture steps…), or Replace picture… with a still." with the two buttons. On the right, the step's properties: **Shown as** (Tooltip beside the hotspot, or Modal card), the **Text**, the **Instruction**, the **Placement** of the tooltip (Auto, Above, Below, Left, Right), the **Chapter**, **Replace picture…** and **Remove hotspot**, and a line with the picture's size (or "No picture yet"), where the picture came from, and whether the step carries a note from the guide. **Transcript** opens a panel under the toolbar with the walkthrough as Markdown, with **Copy** and **Save as…**.

The **player** fills the window. A header carries the walkthrough's name, "Step 3 of 12", a progress bar, a **Chapters** menu when there are chapters, **Transcript**, **Back**, **Next** (reading **Finish** on the last step) and **Close**. Under it, the picture is fitted to the window and centred on a neutral backdrop (a picture smaller than the window is scaled up to fill it); the hotspot is an outlined rectangle that pulses, with the tooltip beside it; a modal step is a centred card over the dimmed picture (a hotspot the step also has is drawn over the dim and still goes on when clicked). A step that has no picture yet is shown as a card on the backdrop with its text and "This step has no picture yet." - never a broken image - and Next goes on as usual. After the last step an end card offers **Restart** and **Close**.

## What to do

### Make a walkthrough

1. Take the stills first: **Capture** → **Capture still** in the desktop app, one still per step. Annotate the ones that need an arrow, a callout, a blur or a crop ([Screen capture](capture.md)): a walkthrough takes the annotated picture when there is one.
2. On **Walkthroughs**, press **New walkthrough**, give it a name and press **Create**. The builder opens.
3. Press **Add step…** and pick a still from the picker: your stills, each with a thumbnail, its name, its size and an **annotated** badge once it is annotated; one click picks it. The still's picture, as it shows now, is copied into the walkthrough as a new step after the selected one (at the end when none is selected), and the new step is selected. Add the rest the same way; a walkthrough holds up to 200 steps, and **Add step…** is greyed out at that.
4. **Draw the hotspot**: drag a rectangle on the picture over the thing the viewer should click. Drag inside it to move it, drag a handle to resize it; it is at least 8 × 8 pixels of the picture and stays inside it. **Remove hotspot** takes it away. Press **Esc** during a drag to put the hotspot back as it was.
5. Write the **Text** (up to 1,000 characters; line breaks are kept) and choose **Shown as**: a **Tooltip** sits beside the hotspot, on the **Placement** you pick, or on the side with room when it is Auto; a **Modal** card is centred over the dimmed picture (Placement is greyed out for it). A tooltip step without a hotspot is shown as a modal card, and the properties panel says so.
6. Write the **Instruction** (up to 2,000 characters; line breaks are kept): what the learner does at this step, as you would say it - "drag the Text file input onto the canvas", "create a hop between the two steps". The text is what the viewer reads on the picture; the instruction is what is said. Read in order, the instructions are the walkthrough's transcript and, later, its narration. A step with no instruction is narrated with its text.
7. Give the first step of each chapter a **Chapter** name (up to 80 characters; the name is trimmed). Every step with a name starts a chapter; the steps before the first named one belong to none. The chapter shows on the step in the list, in the player's **Chapters** menu and as a heading in the transcript.
8. **Reorder** with the arrows on a step, or drag a step and drop it where it should go. **Delete** a step with its bin; it asks first ("Delete step 3? Its picture leaves the walkthrough when you save; the still it was made from is kept.", with **Delete**).
9. **Replace picture…** copies the picture of another still (or the same still after annotating it again) onto the selected step. The step's **Shown as**, text, instruction, placement and chapter stay; the hotspot is kept and brought inside the new picture when that is smaller, or dropped when the picture cannot hold one.
10. **Transcript** (beside Play) shows the walkthrough as Markdown: the name as the title, a heading at each step that starts a chapter, and every step as a numbered item carrying its instruction - its text when it has none, and "(no instruction yet)" when it has neither. **Copy** puts the Markdown on the clipboard and says "Copied to the clipboard." (or "Could not copy: the page may not write to the clipboard." where the page is not allowed to); **Save as…** downloads it as `<name>.md`, the name made safe for a file: only letters, digits, `_`, `.` and `-` are kept, spaces become dashes, at most 60 characters, and `walkthrough.md` when nothing is left. This is the narration script for the narrated video to come.
11. **Save** (Ctrl+S). The whole walkthrough is written in one go, with the revision the builder last took (at opening, and from every save, Add step and Replace picture). Until then the status line reads **Unsaved changes**; afterwards, **Saved**; Ctrl+S with nothing changed answers "Nothing to save." **Undo** and **Redo** (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y; Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes as well) step through every edit of the visit, the hotspot, the texts and the instructions included; a text being typed joins the history when you leave its box. After a save, Undo and Redo no longer reach a version that named a picture the save took away (a step added and then undone before the save): such a version could never be saved again.
12. **Play** opens the player at the selected step. With unsaved changes it asks first ("Save before playing?" with **Save and play**): the player shows what was saved.

Leaving the builder with unsaved changes through the app's own links, or with Back and Forward, asks first ("Leave without saving?" - "The changes you made since the last save are lost if you leave now." - with **Leave** and **Cancel**). In a browser a reload or a closed tab asks through the browser's prompt. **Closing the desktop app's window does not ask: save first.**

If somebody else saved the same walkthrough since you opened it, Save is refused with "This walkthrough was changed elsewhere: reload it." and nothing is written; a **Reload** button beside the message loads the saved walkthrough, asking first when you have unsaved changes ("Reload the walkthrough?" - "Your unsaved changes are lost; the walkthrough as it was last saved is loaded." - with **Reload**), and the status line then reads **Reloaded**. If the walkthrough was deleted meanwhile, or is no longer yours, the builder and the player go back to the list with "That walkthrough is no longer there: it was deleted, or it is not yours."

### Import a workshop

A **course workshop** - a folder of a course as the content manager lays one out: `course.json`, `SUMMARY.md`, `_assets/images/*.png` and one `nn-slug/` folder per workshop holding `manifest.json` and `guide.md` - becomes a walkthrough in one step, from the Walkthroughs page.

1. An administrator points the app at the **Courses folder** once: Settings › **Studio** › **Courses folder**, an existing folder on the server holding one folder per course ([Studio settings](../admin/studio-settings.md)). Until it is set, the import dialog says so and offers nothing. The import only reads the folder; only **Export to course** writes into it - and only while an administrator allows it (*Export to course* below).
2. On **Walkthroughs**, press **Import workshop…**. The dialog lists the **courses** (title and version) and, for the chosen course, its **workshops** as a tick list grouped by section as `SUMMARY.md` orders them, each with its minutes and its step count from the manifest. Tick the workshops the walkthrough is based on (the box's hint: "The walkthrough is based on the workshops ticked, in the course's order; a section's tick takes all of its"): one (the first is ticked when the dialog opens), a whole section - the tick on a section's header takes every workshop of it, and shows half-ticked while only some of them are - or the whole course, up to 50 in one import. The order you tick them in does not matter: the walkthrough follows the course's order. With none ticked there is nothing to import - "Tick at least one workshop." - and Import is greyed out. A course whose `course.json` or `SUMMARY.md` cannot be read - over 2 MB, not UTF-8, not JSON - is left out of the list, as is a workshop whose `manifest.json` or `guide.md` cannot; the server log says which, and the others are listed.
3. The **preview** reads every ticked workshop (all at once) and shows one combined line of what the import would make - "27 steps in 7 chapters; 11 pictures from the guide, 16 to capture" - and, under it, a block per workshop in the course's order: its title, its own line, and its **chapters** as ticks - every `## Section` the parser keeps, and "(before the first section)" for the steps before the first heading - with the steps under each, a camera for a picture from the guide, a crossed-out picture for one to capture. Every chapter is ticked, with how many steps it holds; untick the ones the walkthrough should not have - a section such as "Troubleshooting a bad file", a part you will cover elsewhere - and its steps are left out (the tick's count reads "3 steps - left out", the steps dim): the counts, the workshop's line and the combined line follow (a chapter is ticked by its name, so two sections of one name tick together). A workshop whose guide cannot be read is named in red beside the others' blocks ("02-build-the-pipeline: pdi-2hr-lab/02-build-the-pipeline/guide.md cannot be read as UTF-8 text."), there is no combined line, and Import waits until you untick it; it waits while a guide is still being read, too ("Reading the guides…"). Nothing is written yet. A workshop with no steps - a guide the parser finds none in, or one whose every chapter you unticked - has the line "No steps: the walkthrough will be empty." (a guide with none also says "No steps were found in this guide: the walkthrough will be empty."); a guide whose figure names a PNG in `_assets/images` that is not there says which ("The guide names 1 picture not in the course's _assets (…): those steps import without a picture."); a figure naming anything else is no picture at all (below).
4. Give it a **Name** if you like and press **Import**. Left empty, the name is the workshop's title for one workshop; the course's title when every workshop of the course is ticked; the section's title when every workshop of exactly one titled section is (a course of one section is then the whole course, and the course's title wins); otherwise the title of the first ticked workshop in the course's order and how many more ("Your First Win + 2 more") - the box's placeholder shows it, and its hint says the rule ("When left empty: the workshop's title; for a whole section, the section's; for the whole course, the course's; otherwise the first workshop's title and how many more"). One walkthrough is made on the server - owned by you, like one you built - and opens in the builder with the combined line on its status line ("Imported "See It Work": 27 steps in 7 chapters; 11 pictures from the guide, 16 to capture."). A walkthrough holds at most 200 steps, counted over every workshop ticked together: past that the import is refused as a whole, with nothing written ("The 3 workshops together have more steps than a walkthrough holds (250 of 200)."); untick a chapter or a workshop and try again. A workshop whose guide turns out unreadable, or is no longer there, refuses the whole import the same way, naming it ("pdi-2hr-lab/02-build-the-pipeline/guide.md cannot be read as UTF-8 text.", "No such workshop in that course: 02-build-the-pipeline.").

**Several workshops in one walkthrough.** The steps come in the course's order, each remembering its own workshop (the builder's line under the selected step's picture ends "from pdi-2hr-lab/02-build-the-pipeline", and the **Export to course…** button's tooltip names them all: "Write the figures into pdi-2hr-lab/01-first-win, 02-build-the-pipeline, or download them; or download the interactive player"); the chapters tell the workshops apart: with one workshop they are the guide's sections, as below; with several, each chapter is named "<workshop title> · <section>" ("Your First Win · 2. Preview the valid rows", cut at a word to 80 characters) and a workshop's steps before its first heading start a chapter named by the workshop alone, so every workshop starts a chapter and the player's **Chapters** menu reads like the course. The dialog counts that chapter too: with several workshops ticked, a workshop's line and the combined line count one chapter for its steps before the first heading when you keep them, so the numbers the dialog shows are the import's own. A chapter you unticked is simply not there; the steps you kept keep their place in their guide, so Export to course still finds them. One course per walkthrough: its pictures and its export live in that course's folder. Choosing steps one by one, and reordering the workshops in the dialog, are not offered: the chapter is the unit, and the course's order rules.

What becomes what, by the parser's rules:

- a **chapter** is each `## Section` (or `### Section`) of the guide, in order - except **Troubleshooting**, **Lab Files** and **Solution**, and any section with no step and no launcher; the first step of a section carries the chapter's name. Steps before the first heading belong to no chapter.
- a **step** is each numbered item of a section, in order. A launcher button (`<button data-launch="spoon" data-path="files/win_preview.ktr">Open in Pentaho Data Integration</button>`) is a step too, where it stands: "Open win_preview.ktr in Pentaho Data Integration." (the file's name and the launcher's label from `course.json`; the button's own text when it has no file). The guide's own **View graph** buttons are not steps.
- a step's **instruction** is the item's text as plain text - bold, emphasis and code marks dropped, links reduced to their text, the item's wrapped lines joined, a bullet inside the item a line of its own - followed by what the guide puts after the item before the next item, launcher, hint or heading: a prose paragraph ("A grid appears with 37 of yesterday's orders…"), a bullet list (a line per bullet), a code block (the path or SQL the learner types, verbatim, kept between its ``` fences so the narration to come can skip it) and a table (a line per row, cells joined by " · "); a figure between them does not end it, a hint does. It is capped at 2,000 characters: a paragraph that does not fit is cut after its last whole sentence, a code block that does not fit is left out whole. Every imported text is stripped of control characters and bounded exactly as the builder's save bounds it, so an imported walkthrough always saves as it came.
- a step's **text** (the tooltip) is the instruction's first sentence, at most 140 characters, cut at a word; every imported step is a **Tooltip** (shown as a card until it has a hotspot, as any tooltip step without one).
- a step's **picture** is the first figure that follows the item (or the launcher) before the next item, launcher or heading; a figure after two items in a row belongs to the last of them. A figure is either form a guide uses: a Markdown image line, `![…](../_assets/images/….png)`, or the house figure's image tag, `<img src="../_assets/images/….png" alt="…">`, at the start of its line, inside its `<figure>` or alone (only the opening tags of its `<figure>`, `<div>`, `<p>` or `<a>` may come before it on the line; its attributes in any order, in double, single or no quotes, closed with `>` or `/>`; its other attributes, and the caption and closing tags after it, are not read). Either way the path must name a PNG in the course's `_assets/images`. It is copied into the walkthrough like a still's picture, and the figure's alt text is kept with the step for information. A picture by web address (the guide's video placeholder), an inline `data:` picture, a video and an embedded frame are never a picture; neither is an image in a code block, inside a `<details>` block, in a table cell or in the middle of a sentence. A figure that is no picture by these rules is passed over: the next figure after the item can still be its picture.
- **hints** (the `> **Note:**`, `> **Tip:**`, `> **Warning:**`, `> **Critical:**` and Key Takeaways quotes) are not steps and are left out - except each `> **Under the hood:**` hint, which is kept as the **note** of the step it follows, for the narrated video to come: the player does not show it, the transcript does not say it, and the builder only says a step has one.
- `<details>` blocks, the guide's HTML wrappers and rules are skipped; at most 500 steps are read from one guide, and a walkthrough still holds at most 200.

The guides of the **PDI in 2 Hours** lab, as a measure: *Your First Win* imports as 10 steps in 4 chapters with 2 pictures from the guide and 8 to capture; *Build the Pipeline Yourself* as 23 steps, 9 pictures; *Enrich and Join* as 28 steps, 15 pictures; *Before You Start* as a single launcher step with its welcome-screen picture.

### Capture the steps

A walkthrough with steps that have no picture yet - an imported one, usually - gets its pictures in the **desktop app** as you perform the steps in the application the workshop is about: by default **every click is a step** - the still is taken at the click, the hotspot placed where you clicked, and the next click's still shows the result - and **Shift+F8** takes a still without a click. Nothing drives that application: you do each step, the app takes the still.

1. In the builder press **Capture steps…** (shown in the desktop app only, like the Capture tile). Choose the **kind**, once for every step: **Window** - the window under the pointer when you click or press the key (from the bar's Capture button, the front-most window); **Region** - a region you drag once on the frozen screen, reused for every step; **Full screen** - a monitor (with the clicks on, every click on that monitor is a step, a click on another application there included: use Region or Window to keep other applications out). Leave **Capture on click** ticked ("every click in the frame takes the step's still; Shift+F8 still captures without a click") or untick it for one key press per step. Choose whether the **cursor** is in the picture, and **which steps**: those without a picture (the default), or every step again. Press **Start**. The builder is saved first (as Play saves), then the **capture bar** opens where the recorder bar goes - a small window the screen capture never sees, which you can drag by its grip - showing the walkthrough's name, "Step 3 of 12", the step's text and, smaller, its instruction, and how many steps are to go, and in click mode a second small line, "Click to capture · Shift+F8 captures without a click"; the key is taken and, in click mode, the click hook installed (either without the other if need be), and the walkthrough's window gets out of the way. If the bar cannot be opened the builder says so ("The capture bar could not be opened: …") and nothing starts: no key is taken and the window stays.
2. Do the step in the application. With Capture on click, **the click is the step**: a left or right press anywhere but on the app's own windows (the bar, the walkthrough's window) takes the still at once - the press starts it, not the release - attaches it to the step, and places the **hotspot** round the point you clicked: a 160 × 60 rectangle centred on it, kept inside the picture; a right click is a step like a left one, the menu it opens being the result on the next still. For a step with no click - a key you type, a result you want on its own still - put the pointer on the thing the viewer should click and press **Shift+F8** (or **Capture** on the bar): the same still, the hotspot round the pointer as it was at the moment of the press. The still is taken exactly as **Capture › Capture still** takes one (it lands in Captures too, named "<walkthrough> · step 3"). Three rules on clicks: for a **Region**, a click outside the frame is no step - the bar says "Click inside the frame to capture this step." until the next change - so the rest of the screen stays yours, and for **Full screen** a click off the captured monitor is none either ("Click on the captured screen to capture this step."), while every click ON that monitor is a step, whichever application it lands on; the second press of a **double-click** (within 400 ms and 8 px of the first, with the same button) is not a second step - the first press took the still - while a right-click straight after a left-click is a step of its own; and a click while a still is still being taken is not lost: its still is taken at once beside the first and the two are attached in click order - the bar reads "Capturing…", and its Skip, Back and Done wait, until both have landed - but a third click (or Shift+F8) while both are still in flight is dropped: it claims no step, so the bar stays on its step and says "Too fast: wait for the still." Shift+F8 with the pointer outside the region, or off the captured monitor, takes the still with no hotspot (a click there is no step at all), and so does a capture from the bar's button (the pointer is on the bar then): draw it in the builder afterwards. The bar moves to the next step the moment the click is taken.
3. **Skip** leaves a step for later and moves on; **Back** returns to the step before (a step can be captured again: the new still replaces the picture, the hotspot comes with it). When every step is visited the bar says so.
4. Press **Done**. The bar closes, the key is given back, the window comes back and the builder reloads from the server: every picture attached during the capture is already saved, so nothing reads "Unsaved changes". The status line says how many steps were captured and how many skipped - distinct steps both: a step captured again after Back counts once, a step skipped, Back, skipped again counts once, and a step captured after a skip is not skipped.

**One frame for the run.** For the **Window** kind the run's frame is the widest width and the tallest height of its stills so far - the first still's size to begin with. A later still of a smaller window - a dialog - is drawn centred on a transparent canvas of the frame's size (the player's stage shows through round it, in either theme), with its hotspot moved along, so the pictures drawn on one frame are one size and the player does not jump between them. A still is never scaled: one LARGER than the frame on a side - a maximised window - grows the frame on that side, and is attached at its own size when it is now the frame, or drawn on the grown frame when the frame is larger on the other side; so a run converges on one frame whatever order its windows come in (the earlier stills keep the frame they were drawn on). Start with the main window, so the frame is right from the first still. A Region and a Full screen have one size already.

The key is taken only while the capture runs, as the recording keys are; if another program holds Shift+F8 the bar says so - a red line under the step, "Shift+F8 is taken by another program: click to capture, or use Capture on the bar." (without the tick, "…use Capture on the bar (a capture from the bar has no hotspot).") - and the clicks and its Capture button still work: the click hook is installed whether or not the app has the key. A Windows that cannot hide the bar from the picture gets a red line of its own, and so does a computer where the clicks cannot be hooked ("Clicks could not be captured on this computer: …" - the key and the button still capture, and the "Click to capture" line is not shown); when both the key and the clicks are off, one line names both: "Shift+F8 is taken by another program and clicks could not be captured on this computer: use Capture on the bar (a capture from the bar has no hotspot)." A capture that is refused - no window under the pointer ("No window is under the pointer: point at the window and click, or press Shift+F8."; without the tick, "…point at the window and press Shift+F8."), a still the server could not take - says so in red on the bar as well as on the builder's capture line, until the next change on the bar; a still that fails on its own puts the bar back on its step; one that fails while a later click's still is in flight leaves its step without its new picture, and a red line names it on the bar and the builder's capture line ("Step 6 has no picture: press Back to take it again.", or, with Every step, "Step 6 kept its old picture: press Back to take it again." for a step that had one) until the step gets a picture or you press Done. A capture cannot start while a recording is in flight ("A recording is in progress: stop it first…"). Closing the desktop app's window while the bar is up is allowed - nothing is in flight between captures - and the bar goes with it: the app closes the bar, removes the click hook and gives the key back as the window goes; the pictures already attached are kept.

### Export to course

A walkthrough imported from a course workshop goes back into that workshop - one imported from several workshops into each of them: each step with a picture **Media Studio took** - a still from Capture steps… or Replace picture… - becomes a **figure** in its own workshop's `guide.md`, right after the step it belongs to: the content manager's annotated image - the picture, centred, with a **numbered pin** where the hotspot is and the step's text behind the pin, once the Content Manager draws that form (until then it shows the pin's text as a line: see *The pins show once the Content Manager renders annotated figures* below) - captioned with the step's chapter (the walkthrough's name before the first chapter). The picture itself goes into the course's `_assets/images` as `wt-<workshop>-<walkthrough id>-<step id>.png` - the walkthrough's id in the name, so a walkthrough and its **Duplicate** exported into the same workshop never write over each other's pictures. A step of a nested list item (an item indented under another) gets its figure indented with it, so the figure stays inside that item. A step whose picture is the guide's own is **already in the guide** and gets nothing; a step with no picture yet gets nothing either; a step with a picture but no hotspot gets the plain figure, without a pin. A figure's number - "Step 6" in the dialog's list, and what the pin's text and the picture's alt text fall back to for a step with neither text nor instruction (the caption falls back to it only when the step has no chapter and the walkthrough no name) - is the step's number in the whole walkthrough: in a walkthrough of several workshops the second guide's figures continue from the first's, the way the player counts.

1. In the builder press **Export to course…** - the walkthrough's owner, or an administrator, may export it (with unsaved changes it asks first - "Save before exporting?" with **Save and export**: the export reads the walkthrough as it is saved, as Play does).
2. The dialog reads every workshop's guide **as it is now** and shows what the export would do - nothing is written yet: "3 figures into pdi-2hr-lab/01-first-win/guide.md; 1 step not placed; 2 skipped" - for a walkthrough of several workshops the first line counts the guides ("5 figures into 2 guides; 1 step not placed; 2 skipped") and a block per guide follows with its own line, lists and diff, while a step with a picture of ours that names no workshop (one added from a still) or a workshop the walkthrough was not imported from is listed above them under **Not placed**, since nothing is guessed ("Added in the walkthrough, not imported: with several workshops it names no guide to go into, and none is guessed.", "Its workshop is not one this walkthrough was imported from."; such a step without a picture is skipped like any other, counts in the first line's "skipped" and is listed under **Skipped** in that same block above the guides, with its reason); each placed step with the guide's sentence it goes after, where the pin sits in percent of the picture ("pin at 45.0 %, 23.3 %") and where the figure goes ("after line 62", or "unchanged at line 64" / "rewritten at line 64" for a figure an earlier export put there); the steps **not placed** and why; the **skipped** ones and why ("No picture yet.", "Its picture is the guide's own: already in the guide."); figures an earlier export left that now go ("The step is no longer in the walkthrough."); the guide's own steps that are not in the walkthrough; the files it would write or remove (folded); and the guide's changes as a diff (folded). **Preview again** reads the guide afresh. Tick **Also the interactive player** to put the player beside the guide too (below).
3. With **Allow export into the Courses folder** on (Settings › Studio, an administrator's: [Studio settings](../admin/studio-settings.md)) press **Write to the course**. The pictures are written first, then the player when it is ticked, then each guide in the course's order, each replaced in one go; the dialog then says "Wrote 3 figures into pdi-2hr-lab/01-first-win/guide.md. Review and publish it in the Content Editor." (or "Wrote 22 figures into 2 guides of pdi-2hr-lab (01-first-win, 02-build-the-pipeline). Review and publish them in the Content Editor.") - the course is not published by the export: review the guides in the Content Editor and publish them from there. With the setting off there is no Write button (turned off after the preview, the write answers "Export into the Courses folder is off: …"): press **Download zip** - `pdi-2hr-lab-01-first-win-export.zip` for one workshop, `pdi-2hr-lab-export.zip` for several: the same guides and pictures laid out like the course folder (`01-first-win/guide.md`, `02-build-the-pipeline/guide.md`, `_assets/images/…`) - and copy them into the course by hand; the dialog's file list says what an export would remove, to delete by hand.

**Where a figure goes.** Every imported step remembers where it is in its guide - its section and its first sentence, and which of the guide's steps with those it is - through every picture replaced and every save. The export finds each step's place in the guide as it is now by that, and puts the figure after the step's own lines: the item and what follows it - its prose, its code, its figures - up to the next step, hint, heading, `<details>` block, rule or the end of a tab. A walkthrough imported before this release does not carry that; its steps are found by their first sentences, in order. Either way a step whose item was reworded or removed in the guide since is **not placed** ("Its text is not in the guide any more (the item was reworded, moved or removed)."); a step that no longer says it came from the guide - one added in the builder, or one imported and given a new picture before this release - and whose text is not found says "Added in the walkthrough, not in the guide (or imported before 0.19.0 and its item reworded since)."; and a step the guide gained is listed as in the guide, not in the walkthrough: nothing is guessed. A figure an earlier export put in the guide stays where it is and is rewritten in place.

**Exporting again** changes only what changed: a figure whose step changed (a new picture, a moved hotspot, another chapter after a reorder) is rewritten where it is, with its picture; the figure of a step that has gone, or lost its picture, is taken out with its picture - unless another workshop's guide of the course (one of this walkthrough's own other workshops among them) shows that picture too, or cannot be read to tell (not UTF-8, or larger than a guide may be): then the picture stays and the dialog lists it under **Kept** ("still used by 02-build-the-pipeline", "another guide could not be read (03-schedule-it)"); another walkthrough's figures and pictures and every figure the authors put in the guide themselves are never touched. A doubled block (a copy and paste) is taken back to one. Export twice in a row and the second writes nothing. An export cut short - the app stopped in the middle of a write - can leave its working copies behind (files such as `.guide.md.1a2b3c4d.keep`, `….bak` or `….tmp` beside the guide, in `_assets/images` or in the player's folder) and a picture of this walkthrough that no guide names; the next export lists them under **Left over, swept by the write** ("left by an interrupted export", "a picture of this walkthrough that no guide names") and the write removes them - only files named that way, in the folders this export uses. Each figure sits between two markers in the guide (`<!-- media-studio: walkthrough <id> step <id> -->` … `<!-- /media-studio -->`), invisible on the page: that is how the next export finds it - leave them in, or delete the whole block, markers and all.

**The guide is somebody else's file too.** The write is refused, with nothing written, when a guide changed between the preview and the write - "The guide pdi-2hr-lab/01-first-win/guide.md changed since the preview: preview again." (somebody saved it in the Content Editor meanwhile): press **Preview again**, check, and write. For a walkthrough of several workshops every guide is checked before the first byte, and one changed refuses the whole write; a guide that changes while the pictures are being written is met at the check just before it is replaced, and everything written is taken back. A write that does not carry every guide's sha256 from the preview - a page open since before this release sends one, which is taken for a walkthrough of one workshop only - is refused the same way: "Every guide's sha256 from the preview is needed for a walkthrough of several workshops (02-build-the-pipeline/guide.md is missing): preview again." (a guide the walkthrough was not imported from: "This walkthrough was not imported from the workshop 03-enrich-and-join: preview again."); reload the page and preview again. A guide keeps its line endings (a CRLF guide stays CRLF) and every byte outside the figures; nothing is written outside the course's own folder, and a folder that is a link or a junction is refused ("_assets is a link or a junction: nothing is written through it."). Before the first byte, every file the export writes or removes - in every guide's folder - is checked writable; a read-only guide or picture, or a folder that takes no new file, is refused with the file named - "Could not write pdi-2hr-lab/01-first-win/guide.md into the course: it is read-only. Nothing was changed: download the zip instead." - and a failure met part-way (a file locked by another program, a full disk) takes back everything that export had written before it answers the same way - every guide and every picture, under one journal. Only a write that succeeds leaves an audit row.

**The interactive player.** **Download player** gives the walkthrough as a small offline web page - `walkthrough/index.html` with the player inside it, `walkthrough.json` and the step pictures beside it - that opens in a web browser from the disk, with no network and no app: the same player as Play, its transcript included. It works for any walkthrough, imported or not. With **Also the interactive player** ticked, the write puts the same folder into the workshop (`<workshop>/walkthrough/`, beside the guide - for a walkthrough of several workshops, the FIRST workshop's folder; the workshop's `files` folder is never touched) and adds a line at the end of that guide only - "Interactive walkthrough: open walkthrough/index.html, in this workshop's folder, in a web browser. It runs offline." - until the content manager has a button that opens it inside the guide. Open it from the workshop's folder: inside the Content Manager the link itself does not open the file. The page carries the walkthrough's name and what the player shows; not its owner, its stills or its notes.

A walkthrough that was not imported from a course workshop has nothing to go back into: its dialog says "This walkthrough was not imported from a course workshop: only the player can be exported." and offers **Download player** (the builder's button says so in its tooltip: "Download the interactive player (the walkthrough was not imported from a course workshop)").

**The pins show once the Content Manager renders annotated figures.** The figure is the annotated form the content manager documents, but its guide pane does not draw that form yet: until it is fixed on its side, a guide shows the picture, the pin's text as a numbered line under it, and the caption - readable, without the pin - and its course checker warns that the figure is not the plain house figure (S303). So **export into a course that is published only after that fix**: until then export into a scratch copy, or use Download zip to look; the studio setting **Allow export into the Courses folder** stays off by default.

### Play a walkthrough

Press **Play** on the list (from the first step) or in the builder (from the selected step).

- Click the **hotspot**, press **Next**, **→**, **Space** or **Enter** to go on. On the last step Next reads **Finish** and shows the end card: "That is the end", the walkthrough's name and its number of steps, with **Restart** and **Close**.
- A click on the picture **outside** the hotspot does not go on: it makes the hotspot pulse once more, a hint.
- **Back** or **←** goes back (from the end card, to the last step); **Home** and **End** go to the first and the last step.
- **Chapters** lists the chapters; the current one is marked; a chapter opens at its first step. With the menu open, **↑** and **↓** move within it, wrapping round, and **Enter** opens the chapter.
- **Transcript** in the header, or **T**, opens the transcript in a drawer over the picture - the same Markdown the builder shows - with **Copy** and **Save as…**; the keys for stepping still work under it.
- **Esc** closes what is open first - the transcript, then the Chapters menu - and otherwise leaves the player, as **Close** does: back to the builder when it was opened from there, else to the list.
- The end card's **Restart** starts again at the first step.
- A walkthrough with no steps shows "This walkthrough has no steps yet." with **Close**; the Play buttons are greyed out until there is one, so this is reached only by its address.

The hotspot is a button with a visible focus ring, labelled with the step's text (or "Next step" when it has none), and the tooltip and the card are dialogs named "Step 3 of 12" that are announced to a screen reader. The pulse is left out when the system asks for reduced motion.

### The browser edition

Open the same address in a browser: the list, the builder and the player are all there, and the Captures card lists the stills already taken and opens the annotation editor on them, so a walkthrough can be built, its stills annotated, and played from any machine. Taking a still needs the desktop app on the machine whose screen it is.

## What is to come

Rendering a walkthrough as a **narrated video** is the next phase: each step's narration - its instruction, else its text - read in a chosen voice while the picture zooms to its hotspot, the chapters as chapters, and the notes an import kept from the guide's *Under the hood* hints narrated after their steps if you want them. Sharing a walkthrough (a link, an embed snippet) comes after; the self-contained page is already here - **Download player** in Export to course - and a button in the content manager that opens it inside a workshop's guide is the content manager's to add.

## Under the hood

**A step's picture is a copy, through one copy routine.** Add step copies the picture the still shows at that moment - the same one rule that answers the Captures card, the download and Add to deck (`services.captures.image_path`: the annotated picture once there is one, else the still) - into the walkthrough's own folder. An import copies the guide's picture through the same routine (`_copy_file` in `services/walkthroughs.py`: a temporary file beside the step's, the PNG's header read, the file published atomically as `step-<id>.png`); only where the source comes from differs - a still's from the captures' oracle, a guide's from the course folder, proven under it. Nothing in a walkthrough reads the still or the course again: deleting the still, annotating it afterwards, or editing the course never changes a step. Replace picture copies the still's current picture again. Each step records where its picture came from (`source`: a still's id and whether it was annotated at the time, or the course, the workshop, the guide image, its alt text and the launcher's file - and both, the guide image then empty, once a still replaced an imported step's picture), for information; Export to course reads it to tell a picture of the guide's own from one Media Studio took.

**The files.** A walkthrough lives in `data\walkthroughs\<id>\`: `walkthrough.json` (the record: the id, the name, the owner, when it was made and last saved, the revision, the steps, and for an imported one `source` - the course, the workshop, the course's title and version) and one `step-<id>.png` per step that has a picture beside it. A step without a picture yet has `image` null, a size of 0 × 0 and no hotspot; its first picture goes through Replace picture like any other. Back the folder up with the rest ([Data and backup](../admin/data-and-backup.md)). A walkthrough belongs to whoever made it, like a project: an editor sees their own, an administrator sees everyone's; Duplicate makes a copy owned by whoever pressed it.

**The courses folder is read; only Export to course writes into it.** A course id and a workshop slug are folder names, never paths (no separator, no `..`); the course folder must be a real folder under the courses folder and not a link; every file - `course.json`, `SUMMARY.md`, a manifest, a guide, a guide image - is proven to lie under its folder after each path component is checked not to be a link (a course whose `course.json` is a link is not a course); a guide over 2 MB is refused before it is read; an image that walks out of the course or is not under its `_assets/images`, is a web address (a `data:` source included) or is not a PNG is no picture. `GET /api/courses` lists the courses and their workshops (`configured` false, with where to set the folder, when it is not set), `GET /api/courses/<course>/workshops/<slug>/preview` answers the parse without writing - each step with the chapter it is in (`in_chapter`, `running_chapters`), what the dialog's chapter ticks group by - and `POST /api/walkthroughs/import` makes the walkthrough: from one workshop (`slug`, the body before this release) or from several of one course (`workshops: [{slug, skip_chapters}]`, 1 to `MAX_WORKSHOPS`, the slugs distinct, named in any order; `load_workshops` reads them in the course's order and refuses the lot when one is unreadable or missing, naming it; `import_plan` leaves out the steps of the chapters named, the anchors of the kept steps those of the full guide; `combine_steps` joins the steps and names the chapters across workshops; `create_from_workshop` refuses the lot past `MAX_STEPS` over the total before anything is written (`too_many_steps_message`); `default_name` names the walkthrough when the body does not; `import_source` makes the record's `source` - `{course, slug, title, version, workshops: [{slug, title}, …]}`, `slug` the first workshop's). The parser (`services/workshops.py`) is pure functions over the guide's text, by the rules above; it also gives every step its **span** (the item's or the launcher's first line to the last line that is not blank before the next item, launcher, hint, heading, `<details>`, rule or `:::` fence) and its **anchor** (`anchor_key`: the section's heading without its number and the step's first sentence, each lower case with its spaces collapsed, joined by " :: ", with an ordinal among the guide's steps with that key). The import stores the anchor on each step; Replace picture and every save keep it - the record's own anchor wins, the builder never sends one - and a replaced picture keeps the workshop's `source` with the still's merged in (`image` null: the picture is no longer the guide's).

**Export to course** (`services/course_export.py`) is computed whole before anything is written. `POST /api/walkthroughs/<id>/export/course/plan` (the owner or an administrator; `{"player": true}` for the player too) re-parses the workshop's current guide, places each step by its anchor, else by the longest common subsequence of the steps' first sentences and the guide's (`align`), and answers the placed, the not placed, the skipped, the blocks removed, the guide's own steps not in the walkthrough, the files with what happens to each, the guide's sha256 and the new guide as a unified diff - writing nothing and leaving no audit row. A walkthrough of several workshops is planned per guide: a `GuidePlan` per workshop of the record (`source.workshops`, else the one `source.slug`), in the course's order, held in one `Plan` - the steps grouped by their own `source.slug` (a step with a picture of ours whose slug is none of the record's workshops, or - with several - that names none, is not placed and says why - `FOREIGN_WORKSHOP_REASON`, `NO_WORKSHOP_REASON`; without a picture it is skipped), one guide read, parsed and planned each; the answer carries a block per guide (`guides`: its own placed, not placed, skipped, removed, kept, new-in-guide, sha256 and diff) beside the flat lists, and `workshop`, `guide` and `guide_sha256` name the first guide for a page before this release. The figure is the course standard's house figure (`<div align="center">`, `<figure>`, `<img src alt width>`, `<figcaption>`, one tag a line, no blank line inside) with the content manager's `pcm-annotated` class and one `<li data-x data-y>` at the hotspot's centre in percent of the picture, one decimal, rounded half up in whole numbers (`centre_percent`); its `width` is the picture's pixel width, at most 800 (the course standard divides by the screen's display scale, but a still does not record the scale it was taken at, and most stills are wider than 800 anyway); every text is HTML-escaped and on one line. `POST /api/walkthroughs/<id>/export/course` with `{"guides": [{"slug", "sha256"}, …]}` from the plan (or the one `{"guide_sha256": …}` of a page before this release, for a walkthrough of one workshop) is refused with 403 while the studio setting is off, with 400 when a guide's sha256 is missing from the body or a slug is none of the record's (`SHA_PER_GUIDE_MESSAGE`, `UNKNOWN_GUIDE_MESSAGE`), and with 409, naming the guide, when any guide's sha256 differs (`changed_message`) - every guide's is checked before the first byte; otherwise every folder is proven a real folder inside the course first (no link, no junction) and every target of every guide checked writable (`check_writable`: each folder something goes into takes a new file, each file replaced or removed is not read-only), then the pictures (one `_assets/images` for the course, a picture's name carrying its workshop's slug), the player (beside the first guide) and last each guide in the course's order are written through one routine (`write_file`: a temporary file beside the target, published in one replace; nothing when the bytes are already the same), each guide checked once more just before it is replaced; the pictures of removed figures go last, each only when no other workshop's guide of the course names it - a guide that cannot be read (not UTF-8, over `MAX_GUIDE_BYTES`) or a folder that cannot be listed counts as naming it (`used_elsewhere`) - and last the debris an interrupted export left (`debris_in`: the journal's and `write_file`'s own names, `.<name>.<8 hex>.keep|bak|tmp` and `.ms-write-check-<8 hex>`, in the folders this export uses, plus an orphaned `wt-<slug>-<walkthrough id>-<step id>.png` no guide names), listed in the plan as `debris`. Everything is kept in one journal (the files and folders created, a copy of each file replaced, each file removed set aside under a new name): a failure part-way - an `OSError`, a link met, a guide changed under it - puts the course back as it was, every guide and picture of the export, and answers 400 with the file named (or the 409); the copies are dropped once all is written. A picture's name is `wt-<slug>-<walkthrough id>-<step id>.png` (`image_name`). The guide's new lines take the file's own line ending, and a figure is added as a blank line, the marker, a blank line, the figure, a blank line and the closing marker, so taking a block out restores the guide byte for byte. `GET /api/walkthroughs/<id>/export/course.zip` (`?player=1`) is the same result as a zip - every guide's `guide.md`, the pictures, the player under the first workshop - and `GET /api/walkthroughs/<id>/export/player.zip` the player alone; both are reads, each written to a temporary file (the PNGs stored as they are, the rest deflated), streamed from it and removed once sent. The player is the same `Player` component built on its own (`vite build --mode player`) into one page, `frontend/dist/walkthrough-player.html`, its script and styles inline; the server puts the walkthrough into its data slot and the pictures beside it.

**The guided capture** is the builder's: the shell opens the capture bar in a second mode (`capture_bar_open` with `mode: "steps"`, the same window and page as the recorder's bar, excluded from capture), takes Shift+F8 (`capture_hotkeys_start` with `keys: "steps"`; the recording keys and the capture key are never held together) and, when it is pressed, reads the pointer's position in the shell at that moment - before the page is told, so before any still is taken - in the physical pixels the overlay's regions and the window list are already in, and raises it to the page with the key (`capture:step-key`). With Capture on click (`clicks: true`, the guided capture's key set only - the recorder's never) the same thread installs a low-level mouse hook (`WH_MOUSE_LL`): on a left or right press it reads the point from the hook and hands it to its own queue at once - the hook never eats or delays a click and does nothing else in the callback - and the thread raises `capture:step-click` with the point and the button, unless the point lies inside one of the app's own top-level windows that can take a press (the bar, the overlays, the window: the thread reads them from its own handles on each press - `EnumWindows`, `GetWindowThreadProcessId` against its own process id, then only windows that are shown, not cloaked, not click-through and not minimised, `can_take_press`, by their visible frame, the DWM's extended frame bounds without the invisible resize borders - and never hit-tests the screen, since the window under a point in a Tauri window is WebView2's child, owned by another process; `point_in_any`) or the press repeats the previous raised one within 400 ms and 8 px with the same button (the second press of a double-click; `repeats_press`). The hook is removed when the key is given back (Done, or the window closing). The page treats a click exactly as the key - the same capture, the same hotspot - after the click rule (a click outside a region's frame, or off the chosen monitor in Full screen, is no step: `clickArea`, `clickRule`). Each click claims its step at once (the bar moves on) and takes its still at once - two may be in flight, each a `POST /api/captures` of its own, since the server grabs each into a file of its own - and the attaches land in click order through one promise chain (`MAX_IN_FLIGHT`; a third click meanwhile is "Too fast: wait for the still."). The page takes the still as the Capture dialog does (`POST /api/captures`), attaches it with `POST /api/walkthroughs/<id>/steps/<step>/picture`, whose `hotspot` field sets the hotspot: the 160 × 60 rectangle centred on the pointer minus the capture's origin, clamped into the picture (`hotspotAround` in `lib/guided.ts`), or `null` for a pointer outside the picture or a capture from the bar's button; for the Window kind the request also carries `frame`, the run's frame - the widest width × the tallest height of the stills so far (`frameFor`) - when the still is smaller than it on a side, and the server draws the still centred on a transparent canvas of the frame (`place_in_frame` in `services/walkthroughs.py` places it, never scaled; `_draw_in_frame` draws it inside `_copy_file`, the one copy routine, on the temporary file before that is published, so a still that cannot be drawn leaves nothing behind; a still that does not fit is refused, and the page never sends one - it grows the frame first) and moves the hotspot with it (`hotspot_in_frame`); a frame is bounded by pixels (`MAX_FRAME_PIXELS`, the annotation decode's cap) as well as by a side. Each answer is the builder's saved baseline; Done reloads the record.

**The transcript** is written from the document by one rule on each side - the page's `narrationOf` and the server's `narration_of`: a step says its instruction when that is not blank, else its text; never its note - into the same Markdown, byte for byte (a test on each side holds the same fixture). `GET /api/walkthroughs/<id>/transcript` answers it as `text/markdown`, and as a download named after the walkthrough with `?download=1`; it is a read, so it leaves no audit row. The player's drawer is part of the player itself, built from the document it already holds, so an export of the player will carry it.

**The save is whole and versioned.** Save sends the whole document - the name and the steps, never a picture - with the revision the builder loaded. The server checks the document against the pictures the folder holds (every step names its own picture, `step-<its id>.png`, there at the size it really is; the hotspot in whole pixels, inside the picture, at least 8 × 8; the texts within their limits; the ids unique), refuses it with the reason and writes nothing when it fails, refuses a revision that is not the record's with the "changed elsewhere" line, and otherwise writes the record with the revision raised by one, then removes the pictures no step names any more. Every write to one walkthrough - a save, an added step, a replaced picture, a copy and a delete - runs under that walkthrough's own lock, so two cannot interleave and a delete waits for a save in flight. Adding a step and replacing a picture are the server's own writes and raise the revision too; the builder takes each answer as its new saved state and keeps your other unsaved edits. Duplicate makes a new record at revision 1 and leaves the original as it is. The builder compares what you have with what was saved the way the server stores it - a trailing space in a name or a chapter is no difference - so "Unsaved changes" clears the moment a save answers.

**The player** is one component that takes the document and a function from a step to its picture, and makes no request of its own; the page gives it the API's picture addresses. The picture's displayed rectangle is one calculation (fit inside the window, centred), the hotspot's is the same one applied to its rectangle, and the tooltip's place is another: the side asked, or for Auto the first of below, right, above, left with room for it (or, with room on none, the side with the most), centred on the hotspot and kept on screen.

**The audit log** records `walkthrough.create` (the name), `walkthrough.update` (the step and chapter counts, never a step's text), `walkthrough.add_step` (the still and the position), `walkthrough.replace_picture` (the step and the still, and whether a hotspot was set or removed with it), `walkthrough.duplicate` (which copy of which), `walkthrough.delete` (the name), `walkthrough.import` (the course and the workshop with the step, chapter and picture counts - "pdi-2hr-lab/01-first-win: 10 steps, 4 chapters, 2 pictures", or for several workshops "pdi-2hr-lab: 3 workshops (01-first-win, 02-build-the-pipeline, 03-enrich-and-join): 61 steps, 15 chapters, 26 pictures") and `walkthrough.guided_capture` (its start with the kind and whether every click is a step - "start: region, on click" - its end with how many distinct steps were captured, how many of the captures were by click, and how many steps skipped - "done: 8 captured (7 by click), 1 skipped", the captures made added when a retake made more of them than steps; the stills themselves are `capture.still` rows, and a picture drawn on the run's frame says "framed" in its `walkthrough.replace_picture` row) and `walkthrough.export_course` (a write into a course: "pdi-2hr-lab/01-first-win: 8 figures, 8 images", or for several workshops "pdi-2hr-lab: 22 figures, 22 images into 2 guides (01-first-win, 02-build-the-pipeline)", ", player" when the export included the player; an export that found nothing to write leaves its row too, with 0 images; the plan and the zips leave no row) ([Audit log](../admin/audit-log.md)).

**The limits** - 200 steps (over every workshop of an import together), 50 workshops in one import, 1,000 characters of text, 2,000 of an instruction, 4,000 of a note, 80 of a chapter name, 120 of a walkthrough's name, a hotspot of at least 8 × 8, a save of at most 2 MB, a guide of at most 2 MB, 500 steps read from a guide, a tooltip of 140 characters from the instruction, a captured hotspot of 160 × 60, a double-click's second press within 400 ms and 8 px with the same button, a frame of at most 100 million pixels, two stills in flight at once, an exported figure at most 800 wide, an anchor's key of 400 characters - are in [Limits](../reference/limits.md).
