# Walkthroughs

Turn stills into a click-through walkthrough: each step a picture with a hotspot the viewer clicks, a tooltip or a card, and a chapter; a builder makes one, a player presents it.

A walkthrough is an ordered list of **steps**. Each step is a picture (a still from **Captures**, annotated or not), an optional **hotspot** (a rectangle on the picture the viewer clicks to go on), a **text** shown as a **tooltip** beside the hotspot or as a centred **modal** card, an optional **instruction** - what the learner does at the step, written as it would be spoken ("drag the Text file input onto the canvas") - and an optional **chapter** name that starts a new chapter at that step. A tooltip step with no hotspot has nothing to sit beside, so it is shown as a modal card. Read in order, the instructions are the walkthrough's **transcript**: the narration script of the narrated video to come.

Walkthroughs work in the **browser edition** as in the desktop app: the list, the builder and the player touch no screen. Only **taking a still** needs the desktop app ([Screen capture](capture.md)). A still can come from the **Capture** tile (annotate it if you like, then add it as a step) or, for a walkthrough made from a course workshop, from **Capture steps…** in the builder, which takes one still per step as you perform them.

A walkthrough can also be **imported from a course workshop**: the workshop's sections become the chapters, its numbered steps the steps, each with its instruction and the guide's screenshot where the guide has one; the steps the guide shows no picture for are captured afterwards in the desktop app as you perform them - every click a step, or one key press per step. See *Import a workshop* and *Capture the steps* below.

## What you see

**Walkthroughs** sits in the sidebar after Projects, and the Dashboard has a **Walkthroughs** tile.

The **list** shows a card per walkthrough: the first step's picture (the first step that has one), the name, "12 steps · 3 chapters", when it was last saved and whose it is, and **Open** (the builder), **Play** (greyed out until the walkthrough has a step), **Duplicate** (the copy is named "<name> (copy)", and the page says so) and a bin, **Delete walkthrough**, which asks first ("Delete *name* and its 12 steps? The stills it was made from are kept.", with **Delete**). **New walkthrough** asks for a name and opens the builder; **Import workshop…** makes one from a course workshop (below). When there is none yet, the page says how one is made.

The **builder** has three columns under a toolbar. The toolbar holds the walkthrough's **Name**, **Undo**, **Redo**, **Save**, **Play**, **Transcript**, in the desktop app **Capture steps…**, and a status line that reads **Saved** when the walkthrough is as it was last saved or loaded, **Unsaved changes** whenever it differs, and a one-off line ("Nothing to save.", "Reloaded", "Imported …", or a refusal such as "Could not save: …") until the next change; **Saving…** while a save runs. On the left, the **step list**: a thumbnail and a number for each step (a dashed **No picture** tile for a step that has none yet), the chapter name when the step starts one, arrows to move a step up or down, a bin to delete it (asked first), and **Add step…** at the foot. In the middle, the selected step's picture with its hotspot - or, for a step without a picture, a placeholder: "No picture yet - Capture it in the desktop app (Capture steps…), or Replace picture… with a still." with the two buttons. On the right, the step's properties: **Shown as** (Tooltip beside the hotspot, or Modal card), the **Text**, the **Instruction**, the **Placement** of the tooltip (Auto, Above, Below, Left, Right), the **Chapter**, **Replace picture…** and **Remove hotspot**, and a line with the picture's size (or "No picture yet"), where the picture came from, and whether the step carries a note from the guide. **Transcript** opens a panel under the toolbar with the walkthrough as Markdown, with **Copy** and **Save as…**.

The **player** fills the window. A header carries the walkthrough's name, "Step 3 of 12", a progress bar, a **Chapters** menu when there are chapters, **Transcript**, **Back**, **Next** (reading **Finish** on the last step) and **Close**. Under it, the picture is fitted to the window and centred on a neutral backdrop (a picture smaller than the window is scaled up to fill it); the hotspot is an outlined rectangle that pulses, with the tooltip beside it; a modal step is a centred card over the dimmed picture (a hotspot the step also has is drawn over the dim and still goes on when clicked). A step that has no picture yet is shown as a card on the backdrop with its text and "This step has no picture yet." - never a broken image - and Next goes on as usual. After the last step an end card offers **Restart** and **Close**.

## What to do

### Make a walkthrough

1. Take the stills first: **Capture** → **Capture still** in the desktop app, one still per step. Annotate the ones that need an arrow, a callout, a blur or a crop ([Screen capture](capture.md)): a walkthrough takes the annotated picture when there is one.
2. On **Walkthroughs**, press **New walkthrough**, give it a name and press **Create**. The builder opens.
3. Press **Add step…** and pick a still from the picker: your stills, each with a thumbnail, its name, its size and an **annotated** badge once it is annotated; one click picks it. The still's picture, as it shows now, is copied into the walkthrough as a new step after the selected one (at the end when none is selected), and the new step is selected. Add the rest the same way; a walkthrough holds up to 200 steps, and **Add step…** is greyed out at that.
4. **Draw the hotspot**: drag a rectangle on the picture over the thing the viewer should click. Drag inside it to move it, drag a handle to resize it; it is at least 8 × 8 pixels of the picture and stays inside it. **Remove hotspot** takes it away. Press **Esc** during a drag to put the hotspot back as it was.
5. Write the **Text** (up to 1,000 characters; line breaks are kept) and choose **Shown as**: a **Tooltip** sits beside the hotspot, on the **Placement** you pick, or on the side with room when it is Auto; a **Modal** card is centred over the dimmed picture (Placement is greyed out for it). A tooltip step without a hotspot is shown as a modal card, and the properties panel says so.
6. Write the **Instruction** (up to 2,000 characters; line breaks are kept): what the learner does at this step, as you would say it - "drag the Text file input onto the canvas", "create a hop between the two steps". The text is what the viewer reads on the picture; the instruction is what is said. Read in order, the instructions are the walkthrough's transcript and, later, its narration. A step with no instruction is narrated with its text.
7. Give the first step of each chapter a **Chapter** name (up to 80 characters; the name is trimmed). Every step with a name starts a chapter; the steps before the first named one belong to none. The chapter shows on the step in the list, in the player's **Chapters** menu and as a heading in the transcript.
8. **Reorder** with the arrows on a step, or drag a step and drop it where it should go. **Delete** a step with its bin; it asks first ("Delete step 3? Its picture leaves the walkthrough when you save; the still it was made from is kept.", with **Delete**).
9. **Replace picture…** copies the picture of another still (or the same still after annotating it again) onto the selected step. The step's **Shown as**, text, instruction, placement and chapter stay; the hotspot is kept and brought inside the new picture when that is smaller, or dropped when the picture cannot hold one.
10. **Transcript** (beside Play) shows the walkthrough as Markdown: the name as the title, a heading at each step that starts a chapter, and every step as a numbered item carrying its instruction - its text when it has none, and "(no instruction yet)" when it has neither. **Copy** puts the Markdown on the clipboard and says "Copied to the clipboard." (or "Could not copy: the page may not write to the clipboard." where the page is not allowed to); **Save as…** downloads it as `<name>.md`, the name made safe for a file: only letters, digits, `_`, `.` and `-` are kept, spaces become dashes, at most 60 characters, and `walkthrough.md` when nothing is left. This is the narration script for the narrated video to come.
11. **Save** (Ctrl+S). The whole walkthrough is written in one go, with the revision the builder last took (at opening, and from every save, Add step and Replace picture). Until then the status line reads **Unsaved changes**; afterwards, **Saved**; Ctrl+S with nothing changed answers "Nothing to save." **Undo** and **Redo** (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y; Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes as well) step through every edit of the visit, the hotspot, the texts and the instructions included; a text being typed joins the history when you leave its box. After a save, Undo and Redo no longer reach a version that named a picture the save took away (a step added and then undone before the save): such a version could never be saved again.
12. **Play** opens the player at the selected step. With unsaved changes it asks first ("Save before playing?" with **Save and play**): the player shows what was saved.

Leaving the builder with unsaved changes through the app's own links, or with Back and Forward, asks first ("Leave without saving?" - "The changes you made since the last save are lost if you leave now." - with **Leave** and **Cancel**). In a browser a reload or a closed tab asks through the browser's prompt. **Closing the desktop app's window does not ask: save first.**

If somebody else saved the same walkthrough since you opened it, Save is refused with "This walkthrough was changed elsewhere: reload it." and nothing is written; a **Reload** button beside the message loads the saved walkthrough, asking first when you have unsaved changes ("Reload the walkthrough?" - "Your unsaved changes are lost; the walkthrough as it was last saved is loaded." - with **Reload**), and the status line then reads **Reloaded**. If the walkthrough was deleted meanwhile, or is no longer yours, the builder and the player go back to the list with "That walkthrough is no longer there: it was deleted, or it is not yours."

### Import a workshop

A **course workshop** - a folder of a course as the content manager lays one out: `course.json`, `SUMMARY.md`, `_assets/images/*.png` and one `nn-slug/` folder per workshop holding `manifest.json` and `guide.md` - becomes a walkthrough in one step, from the Walkthroughs page.

1. An administrator points the app at the **Courses folder** once: Settings › **Studio** › **Courses folder**, an existing folder on the server holding one folder per course ([Studio settings](../admin/studio-settings.md)). Until it is set, the import dialog says so and offers nothing; the folder is read and never written.
2. On **Walkthroughs**, press **Import workshop…**. The dialog lists the **courses** (title and version) and, for the chosen course, its **workshops** grouped by section as `SUMMARY.md` orders them, each with its minutes and its step count from the manifest. Pick one. A course whose `course.json` or `SUMMARY.md` cannot be read - over 2 MB, not UTF-8, not JSON - is left out of the list, as is a workshop whose `manifest.json` or `guide.md` cannot; the server log says which, and the others are listed.
3. The **preview** shows what the import would make - "12 steps in 4 chapters; 9 pictures from the guide, 3 to capture" - and the steps themselves, each with its chapter, its text and a camera (a picture from the guide) or a crossed-out picture (to capture). Nothing is written yet. A guide with no steps says "No steps: the walkthrough will be empty."; a guide naming a picture that is not in the course's `_assets` says which, and those steps import without one.
4. Give it a **Name** if you like (the workshop's title otherwise) and press **Import**. The walkthrough is made on the server - owned by you, like one you built - and opens in the builder with the same line on its status line.

What becomes what, by the parser's rules:

- a **chapter** is each `## Section` (or `### Section`) of the guide, in order - except **Troubleshooting**, **Lab Files** and **Solution**, and any section with no step and no launcher; the first step of a section carries the chapter's name. Steps before the first heading belong to no chapter.
- a **step** is each numbered item of a section, in order. A launcher button (`<button data-launch="spoon" data-path="files/win_preview.ktr">Open in Pentaho Data Integration</button>`) is a step too, where it stands: "Open win_preview.ktr in Pentaho Data Integration." (the file's name and the launcher's label from `course.json`; the button's own text when it has no file). The guide's own **View graph** buttons are not steps.
- a step's **instruction** is the item's text as plain text - bold, emphasis and code marks dropped, links reduced to their text, the item's wrapped lines joined, a bullet inside the item a line of its own - followed by what the guide puts after the item before the next item, launcher, hint or heading: a prose paragraph ("A grid appears with 37 of yesterday's orders…"), a bullet list (a line per bullet), a code block (the path or SQL the learner types, verbatim, kept between its ``` fences so the narration to come can skip it) and a table (a line per row, cells joined by " · "); a figure between them does not end it, a hint does. It is capped at 2,000 characters: a paragraph that does not fit is cut after its last whole sentence, a code block that does not fit is left out whole. Every imported text is stripped of control characters and bounded exactly as the builder's save bounds it, so an imported walkthrough always saves as it came.
- a step's **text** (the tooltip) is the instruction's first sentence, at most 140 characters, cut at a word; every imported step is a **Tooltip** (shown as a card until it has a hotspot, as any tooltip step without one).
- a step's **picture** is the first `![…](../_assets/images/….png)` figure that follows the item (or the launcher) before the next item, launcher or heading; a figure after two items in a row belongs to the last of them. It is copied into the walkthrough like a still's picture, and the figure's alt text is kept with the step for information. A picture by web address (the guide's video placeholder) is never a picture.
- **hints** (the `> **Note:**`, `> **Tip:**`, `> **Warning:**`, `> **Critical:**` and Key Takeaways quotes) are not steps and are left out - except each `> **Under the hood:**` hint, which is kept as the **note** of the step it follows, for the narrated video to come: the player does not show it, the transcript does not say it, and the builder only says a step has one.
- `<details>` blocks, the guide's HTML wrappers and rules are skipped; at most 500 steps are read from one guide, and a walkthrough still holds at most 200.

The guides of the **PDI in 2 Hours** lab, as a measure: *Your First Win* imports as 10 steps in 4 chapters with 2 pictures from the guide and 8 to capture; *Build the Pipeline Yourself* as 23 steps, 9 pictures; *Enrich and Join* as 27 steps, 15 pictures; *Before You Start* as a single launcher step with its welcome-screen picture.

### Capture the steps

A walkthrough with steps that have no picture yet - an imported one, usually - gets its pictures in the **desktop app** as you perform the steps in the application the workshop is about: by default **every click is a step** - the still is taken at the click, the hotspot placed where you clicked, and the next click's still shows the result - and **Shift+F8** takes a still without a click. Nothing drives that application: you do each step, the app takes the still.

1. In the builder press **Capture steps…** (shown in the desktop app only, like the Capture tile). Choose the **kind**, once for every step: **Window** - the window under the pointer when you click or press the key (from the bar's Capture button, the front-most window); **Region** - a region you drag once on the frozen screen, reused for every step; **Full screen** - a monitor (with the clicks on, every click on that monitor is a step, a click on another application there included: use Region or Window to keep other applications out). Leave **Capture on click** ticked ("every click in the frame takes the step's still; Shift+F8 still captures without a click") or untick it for one key press per step. Choose whether the **cursor** is in the picture, and **which steps**: those without a picture (the default), or every step again. Press **Start**. The builder is saved first (as Play saves), then the **capture bar** opens where the recorder bar goes - a small window the screen capture never sees, which you can drag by its grip - showing the walkthrough's name, "Step 3 of 12", the step's text and, smaller, its instruction, and how many steps are to go, and in click mode a second small line, "Click to capture · Shift+F8 captures without a click"; the key is taken and, in click mode, the click hook installed (either without the other if need be), and the walkthrough's window gets out of the way. If the bar cannot be opened the builder says so ("The capture bar could not be opened: …") and nothing starts: no key is taken and the window stays.
2. Do the step in the application. With Capture on click, **the click is the step**: a left or right press anywhere but on the app's own windows (the bar, the walkthrough's window) takes the still at once - the press starts it, not the release - attaches it to the step, and places the **hotspot** round the point you clicked: a 160 × 60 rectangle centred on it, kept inside the picture; a right click is a step like a left one, the menu it opens being the result on the next still. For a step with no click - a key you type, a result you want on its own still - put the pointer on the thing the viewer should click and press **Shift+F8** (or **Capture** on the bar): the same still, the hotspot round the pointer as it was at the moment of the press. The still is taken exactly as **Capture › Capture still** takes one (it lands in Captures too, named "<walkthrough> · step 3"). Three rules on clicks: for a **Region**, a click outside the frame is no step - the bar says "Click inside the frame to capture this step." until the next change - so the rest of the screen stays yours, and for **Full screen** a click off the captured monitor is none either ("Click on the captured screen to capture this step."), while every click ON that monitor is a step, whichever application it lands on; the second press of a **double-click** (within 400 ms and 8 px of the first, with the same button) is not a second step - the first press took the still - while a right-click straight after a left-click is a step of its own; and a click while a still is still being taken is not lost: its still is taken at once beside the first and the two are attached in click order - the bar reads "Capturing…", and its Skip, Back and Done wait, until both have landed - but a third click (or Shift+F8) while both are still in flight is dropped: it claims no step, so the bar stays on its step and says "Too fast: wait for the still." Shift+F8 with the pointer outside the region, or off the captured monitor, takes the still with no hotspot (a click there is no step at all), and so does a capture from the bar's button (the pointer is on the bar then): draw it in the builder afterwards. The bar moves to the next step the moment the click is taken.
3. **Skip** leaves a step for later and moves on; **Back** returns to the step before (a step can be captured again: the new still replaces the picture, the hotspot comes with it). When every step is visited the bar says so.
4. Press **Done**. The bar closes, the key is given back, the window comes back and the builder reloads from the server: every picture attached during the capture is already saved, so nothing reads "Unsaved changes". The status line says how many steps were captured and how many skipped - distinct steps both: a step captured again after Back counts once, a step skipped, Back, skipped again counts once, and a step captured after a skip is not skipped.

**One frame for the run.** For the **Window** kind the run's frame is the widest width and the tallest height of its stills so far - the first still's size to begin with. A later still of a smaller window - a dialog - is drawn centred on a transparent canvas of the frame's size (the player's stage shows through round it, in either theme), with its hotspot moved along, so the pictures drawn on one frame are one size and the player does not jump between them. A still is never scaled: one LARGER than the frame on a side - a maximised window - grows the frame on that side, and is attached at its own size when it is now the frame, or drawn on the grown frame when the frame is larger on the other side; so a run converges on one frame whatever order its windows come in (the earlier stills keep the frame they were drawn on). Start with the main window, so the frame is right from the first still. A Region and a Full screen have one size already.

The key is taken only while the capture runs, as the recording keys are; if another program holds Shift+F8 the bar says so - a red line under the step, "Shift+F8 is taken by another program: click to capture, or use Capture on the bar." (without the tick, "…use Capture on the bar (a capture from the bar has no hotspot).") - and the clicks and its Capture button still work: the click hook is installed whether or not the app has the key. A Windows that cannot hide the bar from the picture gets a red line of its own, and so does a computer where the clicks cannot be hooked ("Clicks could not be captured on this computer: …" - the key and the button still capture, and the "Click to capture" line is not shown); when both the key and the clicks are off, one line names both: "Shift+F8 is taken by another program and clicks could not be captured on this computer: use Capture on the bar (a capture from the bar has no hotspot)." A capture that is refused - no window under the pointer ("No window is under the pointer: point at the window and click, or press Shift+F8."; without the tick, "…point at the window and press Shift+F8."), a still the server could not take - says so in red on the bar as well as on the builder's capture line, until the next change on the bar; a still that fails on its own puts the bar back on its step; one that fails while a later click's still is in flight leaves its step without its new picture, and a red line names it on the bar and the builder's capture line ("Step 6 has no picture: press Back to take it again.", or, with Every step, "Step 6 kept its old picture: press Back to take it again." for a step that had one) until the step gets a picture or you press Done. A capture cannot start while a recording is in flight ("A recording is in progress: stop it first…"). Closing the desktop app's window while the bar is up is allowed - nothing is in flight between captures - and the bar goes with it: the app closes the bar, removes the click hook and gives the key back as the window goes; the pictures already attached are kept.

### Play a walkthrough

Press **Play** on the list (from the first step) or in the builder (from the selected step).

- Click the **hotspot**, press **Next**, **→**, **Space** or **Enter** to go on. On the last step Next reads **Finish** and shows the end card: "That is the end", the walkthrough's name and its number of steps, with **Restart** and **Close**.
- A click on the picture **outside** the hotspot does not go on: it makes the hotspot pulse once more, a hint.
- **Back** or **←** goes back (from the end card, to the last step); **Home** and **End** go to the first and the last step.
- **Chapters** lists the chapters; the current one is marked; a chapter opens at its first step. With the menu open, **↑** and **↓** move within it, wrapping round, and **Enter** opens the chapter.
- **Transcript** in the header, or **T**, opens the transcript in a drawer over the picture - the same Markdown the builder shows - with **Copy** and **Save as…**; the keys for stepping still work under it.
- **Esc** closes what is open first - the transcript, then the Chapters menu - and otherwise leaves the player, as **Close** does: back to the builder when it was opened from there, else to the list.
- The end card's **Restart** starts again at the first step.
- A walkthrough with no steps shows "This walkthrough has no steps yet." with **Close**; the Play buttons are greyed out until there is one, so this is reached only by its address.

The hotspot is a button with a visible focus ring, labelled with the step's text (or "Next step" when it has none), and the tooltip and the card are dialogs named "Step 3 of 12" that are announced to a screen reader. The pulse is left out when the system asks for reduced motion.

### The browser edition

Open the same address in a browser: the list, the builder and the player are all there, and the Captures card lists the stills already taken and opens the annotation editor on them, so a walkthrough can be built, its stills annotated, and played from any machine. Taking a still needs the desktop app on the machine whose screen it is.

## What is to come

Rendering a walkthrough as a **narrated video** is the next phase: each step's narration - its instruction, else its text - read in a chosen voice while the picture zooms to its hotspot, the chapters as chapters, and the notes an import kept from the guide's *Under the hood* hints narrated after their steps if you want them. Sharing a walkthrough (a link, an embed snippet, an export as a self-contained HTML page) comes after; the player is written so that an export can carry it unchanged.

## Under the hood

**A step's picture is a copy, through one copy routine.** Add step copies the picture the still shows at that moment - the same one rule that answers the Captures card, the download and Add to deck (`services.captures.image_path`: the annotated picture once there is one, else the still) - into the walkthrough's own folder. An import copies the guide's picture through the same routine (`_copy_file` in `services/walkthroughs.py`: a temporary file beside the step's, the PNG's header read, the file published atomically as `step-<id>.png`); only where the source comes from differs - a still's from the captures' oracle, a guide's from the course folder, proven under it. Nothing in a walkthrough reads the still or the course again: deleting the still, annotating it afterwards, or editing the course never changes a step. Replace picture copies the still's current picture again. Each step records where its picture came from (`source`: a still's id and whether it was annotated at the time, or the course, the workshop, the guide image, its alt text and the launcher's file), for information only.

**The files.** A walkthrough lives in `data\walkthroughs\<id>\`: `walkthrough.json` (the record: the id, the name, the owner, when it was made and last saved, the revision, the steps, and for an imported one `source` - the course, the workshop, the course's title and version) and one `step-<id>.png` per step that has a picture beside it. A step without a picture yet has `image` null, a size of 0 × 0 and no hotspot; its first picture goes through Replace picture like any other. Back the folder up with the rest ([Data and backup](../admin/data-and-backup.md)). A walkthrough belongs to whoever made it, like a project: an editor sees their own, an administrator sees everyone's; Duplicate makes a copy owned by whoever pressed it.

**The courses folder is read and nothing else.** A course id and a workshop slug are folder names, never paths (no separator, no `..`); the course folder must be a real folder under the courses folder and not a link; every file - `course.json`, `SUMMARY.md`, a manifest, a guide, a guide image - is proven to lie under its folder after each path component is checked not to be a link (a course whose `course.json` is a link is not a course); a guide over 2 MB is refused before it is read; an image that walks out of the course, is a web address or is not a PNG is no picture. `GET /api/courses` lists the courses and their workshops (`configured` false, with where to set the folder, when it is not set), `GET /api/courses/<course>/workshops/<slug>/preview` answers the parse without writing, and `POST /api/walkthroughs/import` makes the walkthrough. The parser (`services/workshops.py`) is pure functions over the guide's text, by the rules above.

**The guided capture** is the builder's: the shell opens the capture bar in a second mode (`capture_bar_open` with `mode: "steps"`, the same window and page as the recorder's bar, excluded from capture), takes Shift+F8 (`capture_hotkeys_start` with `keys: "steps"`; the recording keys and the capture key are never held together) and, when it is pressed, reads the pointer's position in the shell at that moment - before the page is told, so before any still is taken - in the physical pixels the overlay's regions and the window list are already in, and raises it to the page with the key (`capture:step-key`). With Capture on click (`clicks: true`, the guided capture's key set only - the recorder's never) the same thread installs a low-level mouse hook (`WH_MOUSE_LL`): on a left or right press it reads the point from the hook and hands it to its own queue at once - the hook never eats or delays a click and does nothing else in the callback - and the thread raises `capture:step-click` with the point and the button, unless the point lies inside one of the app's own top-level windows that can take a press (the bar, the overlays, the window: the thread reads them from its own handles on each press - `EnumWindows`, `GetWindowThreadProcessId` against its own process id, then only windows that are shown, not cloaked, not click-through and not minimised, `can_take_press`, by their visible frame, the DWM's extended frame bounds without the invisible resize borders - and never hit-tests the screen, since the window under a point in a Tauri window is WebView2's child, owned by another process; `point_in_any`) or the press repeats the previous raised one within 400 ms and 8 px with the same button (the second press of a double-click; `repeats_press`). The hook is removed when the key is given back (Done, or the window closing). The page treats a click exactly as the key - the same capture, the same hotspot - after the click rule (a click outside a region's frame, or off the chosen monitor in Full screen, is no step: `clickArea`, `clickRule`). Each click claims its step at once (the bar moves on) and takes its still at once - two may be in flight, each a `POST /api/captures` of its own, since the server grabs each into a file of its own - and the attaches land in click order through one promise chain (`MAX_IN_FLIGHT`; a third click meanwhile is "Too fast: wait for the still."). The page takes the still as the Capture dialog does (`POST /api/captures`), attaches it with `POST /api/walkthroughs/<id>/steps/<step>/picture`, whose `hotspot` field sets the hotspot: the 160 × 60 rectangle centred on the pointer minus the capture's origin, clamped into the picture (`hotspotAround` in `lib/guided.ts`), or `null` for a pointer outside the picture or a capture from the bar's button; for the Window kind the request also carries `frame`, the run's frame - the widest width × the tallest height of the stills so far (`frameFor`) - when the still is smaller than it on a side, and the server draws the still centred on a transparent canvas of the frame (`place_in_frame` in `services/walkthroughs.py` places it, never scaled; `_draw_in_frame` draws it inside `_copy_file`, the one copy routine, on the temporary file before that is published, so a still that cannot be drawn leaves nothing behind; a still that does not fit is refused, and the page never sends one - it grows the frame first) and moves the hotspot with it (`hotspot_in_frame`); a frame is bounded by pixels (`MAX_FRAME_PIXELS`, the annotation decode's cap) as well as by a side. Each answer is the builder's saved baseline; Done reloads the record.

**The transcript** is written from the document by one rule on each side - the page's `narrationOf` and the server's `narration_of`: a step says its instruction when that is not blank, else its text; never its note - into the same Markdown, byte for byte (a test on each side holds the same fixture). `GET /api/walkthroughs/<id>/transcript` answers it as `text/markdown`, and as a download named after the walkthrough with `?download=1`; it is a read, so it leaves no audit row. The player's drawer is part of the player itself, built from the document it already holds, so an export of the player will carry it.

**The save is whole and versioned.** Save sends the whole document - the name and the steps, never a picture - with the revision the builder loaded. The server checks the document against the pictures the folder holds (every step names its own picture, `step-<its id>.png`, there at the size it really is; the hotspot in whole pixels, inside the picture, at least 8 × 8; the texts within their limits; the ids unique), refuses it with the reason and writes nothing when it fails, refuses a revision that is not the record's with the "changed elsewhere" line, and otherwise writes the record with the revision raised by one, then removes the pictures no step names any more. Every write to one walkthrough - a save, an added step, a replaced picture, a copy and a delete - runs under that walkthrough's own lock, so two cannot interleave and a delete waits for a save in flight. Adding a step and replacing a picture are the server's own writes and raise the revision too; the builder takes each answer as its new saved state and keeps your other unsaved edits. Duplicate makes a new record at revision 1 and leaves the original as it is. The builder compares what you have with what was saved the way the server stores it - a trailing space in a name or a chapter is no difference - so "Unsaved changes" clears the moment a save answers.

**The player** is one component that takes the document and a function from a step to its picture, and makes no request of its own; the page gives it the API's picture addresses. The picture's displayed rectangle is one calculation (fit inside the window, centred), the hotspot's is the same one applied to its rectangle, and the tooltip's place is another: the side asked, or for Auto the first of below, right, above, left with room for it (or, with room on none, the side with the most), centred on the hotspot and kept on screen.

**The audit log** records `walkthrough.create` (the name), `walkthrough.update` (the step and chapter counts, never a step's text), `walkthrough.add_step` (the still and the position), `walkthrough.replace_picture` (the step and the still, and whether a hotspot was set or removed with it), `walkthrough.duplicate` (which copy of which), `walkthrough.delete` (the name), `walkthrough.import` (the course and the workshop with the step, chapter and picture counts) and `walkthrough.guided_capture` (its start with the kind and whether every click is a step - "start: region, on click" - its end with how many distinct steps were captured, how many of the captures were by click, and how many steps skipped - "done: 8 captured (7 by click), 1 skipped", the captures made added when a retake made more of them than steps; the stills themselves are `capture.still` rows, and a picture drawn on the run's frame says "framed" in its `walkthrough.replace_picture` row) ([Audit log](../admin/audit-log.md)).

**The limits** - 200 steps, 1,000 characters of text, 2,000 of an instruction, 4,000 of a note, 80 of a chapter name, 120 of a walkthrough's name, a hotspot of at least 8 × 8, a save of at most 2 MB, a guide of at most 2 MB, 500 steps read from a guide, a tooltip of 140 characters from the instruction, a captured hotspot of 160 × 60, a double-click's second press within 400 ms and 8 px with the same button, a frame of at most 100 million pixels, two stills in flight at once - are in [Limits](../reference/limits.md).
