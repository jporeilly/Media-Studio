## [Unreleased]

- [feat] **A deck renders several times faster, and a transition or the watermark no longer multiplies the render time: ffmpeg now builds the video from the slides' still images. Fade to White and the four slide directions now look as their names say, an animated slide plays for as long as its narration at 24 frames a second, and a deck's narration is about 3 dB louder, at the level it was synthesised at.** moviepy used to build every frame in Python and pipe it to ffmpeg; any transition raised the whole video to 24 frames a second and the watermark was composited onto every frame, so the owner's 20-slide deck (7½ minutes of narration, 1080p) took 105 s with no transition and 42 minutes with a transition and the watermark on 0.11.0. Now Python draws the title cards and the watermark once each with Pillow and builds the narration track as before, and ffmpeg scales each slide's image once, repeats it in memory, draws the transitions with its own filters and encodes; nothing in the app imports moviepy any more. On that deck, rendered through the code on an 8-core desktop with the ffmpeg the app ships: 25.0 to 25.2 s with no transition (115.8 s on the old renderer, same machine), 49.3 to 51.5 s with a crossfade and the watermark (2.0 to 2.1 times the render with no transition), 65.0 to 66.3 s with Zoom In and the watermark, and 1.9 s for the 15-second preview. Four of its slides with a crossfade and the watermark took 472.9 s before and 9.3 s now (the new figure timed with ffmpeg 8.0.1, not the 7.1 the app ships). The video is encoded in parts (up to 8 slides and pauses at 1080p, 18 at 720p, 4 at 4K), each one ffmpeg run, joined by a stream copy that keeps every frame in order, because one filter graph over a whole deck holds every slide in memory at once: with a crossfade, ffmpeg peaked at 3.0 GB as one graph and 1.4 GB in parts at 1080p, 10.0 GB and 4.0 GB at 4K, and 7.2 GB against 1.4 GB for a 60-slide deck, which in parts needs what a 20-slide deck does. The narration and the music are encoded by their own ffmpeg run beside the picture's. Fade to White fades each slide in from white and out to white (it never faded in, and cut to black before it turned white); Slide Left enters from the right edge and Slide Up from the bottom (they moved like Slide Right and Slide Down). An animated slide is repeated or cut to its narration's span (it played its own length, so every later slide drifted from its narration: 2.0 s after a 4 s clip under 2 s of narration) and raises the deck to 24 frames a second (at 2 it showed two pictures a second). The narration is up-mixed to stereo at unity like a re-voice's: 0.00 dB against its source, where moviepy's reader had ffmpeg up-mix the mono track with `-ac 2`, 3.02 dB down; no sample reaches full scale, with or without music at the default volume. Segment boundaries are rounded to frames on the cumulative time, the picture follows the gaps the narration track really leaves, and it never ends before the narration does (its last frame rounds up; rounding to the nearest frame cut up to half a frame of the last words). Every transition that fades or zooms now opens dark on every slide: in the first build of this release, a slide whose start rounded down to a frame began a fraction of a frame early, and ffmpeg's fade never fades from a negative timestamp, so 8 of this deck's 19 fading slides cut in at full brightness; every transition now runs on a clock that starts a second in. The watermark sits on exactly the pixel it is placed at (the overlay snapped an odd coordinate to the even one before it, a row too high on this deck). Everything else matches the old renderer frame against frame, on slides whose start falls on either side of a frame: the slides, the title cards and the pauses at SSIM 0.9954 to 1.0000; the watermark on a dark corner, where it shows, at 0.9999 and 52.6 dB; and Fade to Black, Crossfade, Slide Right, Slide Down and Zoom In through their transitions at 0.978 to 1.0000. The one exception is a fade-out on a dark slide (0.92 to 0.97): the old renderer truncated the faded values and came out up to a level darker than the true fade, and the new one is nearer it. Chapters and subtitles are unchanged. — #t1

- [feat] **A deck's render, and its 15-second preview, can be cancelled: the Generate card shows Cancel while its job runs, to the user who started it and to an administrator.** `POST /api/jobs/{id}/cancel` on a render used to answer 200 while the render ran to its end: the processor honoured a cancel flag at every stage and nothing ever set it. The generate job now binds the check to itself in its own thread (`services.jobs.cancel_check_here`; the narration is synthesised on a thread pool, where a question about the calling thread finds no job) and hands it to the processor, which looks at it before and after the slide export (PowerPoint is not interrupted mid-export), before every slide it narrates and before each retry of a failed one, while ffmpeg encodes (killed within about half a second; the `.part.mp4` removed, the previous video untouched), before the subtitles and before and during each extra format — each WebM, GIF and MP3 is now one ffmpeg run written to a part file, published only when whole and killed by a cancel the way the encode is (before this a format was written straight under its final name and recorded whenever a file was there, so a run that failed part-way left a half-written file the record named and served). The job ends `done` with `result = {"cancelled": true, "stage": "export" | "narration" | "encode" | "subtitles" | "formats"}` and a closing line the card keeps: stopped before the new video is written, "Render cancelled before the video was written; the project is as it was." (a preview: "Preview cancelled before it was written; the previous preview is as it was.") — the record is not touched (but for where the slide images came from, when the export ran) and the previous video and every sidecar it names are byte for byte what they were, while the slide images and the narration clips already made are kept for the next render; stopped after it, a line that names what was finished and what was not (for per-slide subtitles, a WebM and a GIF asked for and the cancel during the WebM: "Render cancelled once the video was written: the new video is kept, with its subtitles; the WebM and the GIF were not written. Render again for the full set.") — the new video is recorded with the sidecars that were finished, the unfinished ones absent from the record and from disk, and the previous render's file of each kind asked for and not produced is deleted (as it now is for a format that fails on a render that completes, and for the previous subtitles when the mode changes), so no stale sidecar sits beside the new video; a kind not asked for keeps its file on disk. A cancel is never reported as a failure, nor a failure as a cancel. Measured through the routes on the dev box's ffmpeg 8.0.1 and the bundled 7.1: from the cancel to the job's end, 0.5 s during the encode and during a WebM, 0.5 s for a preview, 0.8 s during the narration (the sentences in flight are not interrupted; no further one starts) and 0.3 s during a failed sentence's retry wait. The Slides, Generate and Re-voice cards now share the page's one cancel request; a job someone else started is still shown read-only (Cancel is its starter's or an administrator's), and a refused cancel says so under its own job. — #t2

Renders are now encoded to be delivered. A full render uses x264 `medium` and H.264 High at
4:2:0, AAC given 192 kbit/s and the index at the front of the file, so a video on the web
starts playing before it has all arrived: on a ten-slide deck the picture came out 4.5
times smaller for 6 % more render time. The 15-second preview stays fast. A re-voiced
video's sound is now 48 kHz stereo AAC given 192 kbit/s, where it was 24 kHz at 97 to
132 kbit/s; its narration track is a 160 kbit/s MP3, and a cut of the picture declares
the H.264 level it needs. A
fresh install uses `gemma4:12b` as its Ollama model, and every Ollama request asks the

- [feat] **A full render is now encoded as its label says: x264 `medium`, H.264 High, 4:2:0, AAC given 192 kbit/s and the index at the front of the file; the preview stays fast, and a re-voice carries its sound at 48 kHz.** Every deck video used to be libx264 `ultrafast` with no profile and no audio bitrate: `ultrafast` turns off CABAC, B-frames and the 8×8 transform, so x264 flagged the stream Constrained Baseline and the file was several times larger than it needed to be, with AAC at the encoder's default of about 128 kbit/s and the index after the media (the re-voice's music pass already wrote it first). Each output preset now carries `x264_preset` (`medium`), `profile` (`high`) and `audio_bitrate`, threaded from the generate route through `VideoProcessor` into `VideoCreator.encode_settings()`, which `create_video` hands to `write_videofile` with `-profile:v high -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart`; the chapter remux that follows keeps the index at the front. Every number in a description is what the encoder is given ("1920×1080 for YouTube; H.264 High, AAC 192 kbps."; Vimeo's "at a 10 Mbps target", which a deck's repeated frames read far below). Measured before choosing, on a ten-slide deck with 14 minutes of narration at 1080p: the whole render 198 s at `ultrafast` and 209 s at `medium` (1.06×, well inside the 4× rule; building the frames dominates either), the video stream 185 kbit/s down to 41 at the same measured quality; three slides with a crossfade at 24 fps, 327 s against 329 s. **Preview 15 s** keeps `ultrafast` with the same parameters, so it stays quick and plays wherever the full render does. The re-voice matches. A cut picture is encoded at `medium`, High, 4:2:0 and told the recording's frame rate, so it declares the H.264 level its size and rate need (4.0 for 1080p at 30; it declared 6.2) with every frame and timestamp kept; it takes about 33 s for the 341 s corpus recording, twice the old time, so the **Render** line now estimates `5 + reach × 0.1` seconds ("about 40 s" for that edit). A re-voice's narration used to reach the mux as a 32 kbit/s MP3 (pydub's default at the engines' 24 kHz) and leave it as 24 kHz AAC at about 100 kbit/s whatever it was given; it now reaches the mux as a WAV, and the mux and the music pass up-mix it to stereo at unity, resample to 48 kHz and give AAC 192 kbit/s, one named constant for both: a narrated re-voice reads at or a little under that, lower over silence (182–193 kbit/s across the eight paths of the Review's 12 s test recording, 12 % silence; 122.8 kbit/s for the 341 s corpus recording, 43 % silence), and its standalone narration track is an MP3 at 160 kbit/s. A re-voice with no cut still copies the source's picture. The audio is given 192 kbit/s rather than YouTube's 384 or Vimeo's 320: ffmpeg's AAC encoder writes about 210 kbit/s of narration and 257 of music whatever higher figure it is given, so those labels would have described files the app never makes. `tests/test_encode_settings.py` renders a synthetic deck through `create_video`, and a re-voice through its route (cut, mux, music and chapters), on each real ffmpeg and reads the files back with ffprobe. — #q1

- [feat] **A fresh install uses `gemma4:12b` as its Ollama model, and every Ollama request now asks the model not to think.** The default in `utils/config.py` changes from `gemma3:12b`; an install that has ever saved its settings keeps its saved model (`data/config.json` wins over the defaults), so an upgrade switches no one; change it in Settings › Studio › Ollama model. Every request to `/api/generate` and `/api/chat` now sends `"think": false`, built by one helper, `core/ollama_client.request_body`. gemma4 thinks by default: on this machine (Ollama 0.34.4) one rewritten sentence took 16 s with thinking and came back as a Markdown list of options, and 1.4 s as one sentence without it. Models that cannot think accept the flag and ignore it (checked on gemma3:12b, llama3.2:3b and qwen2.5:0.5b); the qwen3 models in the catalogue stop thinking too, so they answer faster and show no thinking text. `gemma4:12b` joins the catalogue (All-Rounder, Vision, Chat), and the assistant counts gemma4 as a vision family, so it is sent the slide images. A new test, `tests/test_ollama_request_body.py`, fails if any module in `core/`, `services/`, `api/` or `utils/` calls either endpoint without the helper or builds the body as a dict literal. On a 20-slide PDI deck with speaker notes and PowerPoint previews (vision on, same slides), AI Enhance averaged 5.6 s a slide on gemma4:12b against 8.6 s on gemma3:12b, and gemma4 kept closer to the original notes; QA review of the clean deck scored gemma4 9/10 with no issues and gemma3 7/10 with 10 mostly stylistic flags. With three errors seeded, both found all three, but only gemma4 also caught the second error in two sentences, and it flagged no clean slide where gemma3 flagged two. One weakness seen: asked directly, gemma4 read a small "1" in a dialog screenshot as blank, which gemma3 read correctly. — #gemma4

- [fix] **A job the server lost no longer freezes a page, and the project page and the Settings page follow the job in flight whoever started it.** Jobs live in memory, so after a backend restart a polled job answered 404 "Job not found.", nothing read the poll's error, the card froze on its last progress, every other card kept waiting and the page polled every 1.2 s until a reload. Once the server answers again, the page follows the job again if it still runs, also after a flap, or asks after it once more, so a job lost in the restart gets the restart line. A job whose polls the server itself refuses gets one more try and then keeps its line, so nothing loops. A job that has ended is not refetched when the browser reconnects. - [feat] **A music clip whose file has left the library can now be trimmed shorter from either end and split, never lengthened: "you may keep what you have, and you may keep less of it"; a cut of the picture across one is refused first, so every cut made while a file is missing stays undoable** (porting vertical 6, **E6** — the owner's decisions of 2026-09-29, relaxing E4c's rule, and 2026-09-30, after the review). Since 0.10.0 such a clip could be moved, levelled, faded and removed, but its slice was frozen: the strip drew it with no trim edges, and a cut or a split across it was refused before anything was sent. **The rule now** (`services/edit.py::_check_music`): a clip naming a missing file is accepted if and only if the project already holds a clip of the same file whose slice **contains** the new one (`in` no earlier, `out` no later, to the millisecond), under the stored id — a trim — or a new one — the second half of a split. Refused: a slice that grows past every slice the project holds of that file, at either end, by as little as a millisecond (*file 'x' is not in the library, so its slice can shrink but not grow; it was 0.500–4.500 of the file*, or *the project holds 0.000–2.000 and 5.000–9.000 of the file* for a new id, then the ways out), and any clip naming a missing file the project never held (the sentence 0.9.0 used). **On the strip** a missing clip has both trim edges, bounded by the slice it holds — its `in` may rise and its `out` fall, never back past them (`lib/edit.ts::clipAt`, `trimClip`; before, the left edge would have grown down to the top of the file) — and **S** splits it like any other clip. **A cut of the picture that would shorten, split or remove a missing clip is refused before anything is sent** — a range cut with Video and Music unlocked, or a piece's edge dragged in across it — because the server would store the cut but not its undo, which grows the clip back, and the picture's cut would go with the refused undo: *"That cut (0:02.000 – 0:03.500) would cut across gone.mp3, but its file is no longer in the library, so the cut could not be undone while it is gone. Lock the Music lane to cut the picture alone, or remove the missing clip first."* (`lib/edit.ts::missingCutByPicture` and `pictureCutRefusal`, in `useEditGestures.ts::cutSelection` and the root's piece-trim release; E4c's `missingAcross` is gone). A cut that only moves a missing clip along goes through as ever. The banner, the clip's title and the inspector say what is still refused (*trimmed shorter or split, but never lengthened past the part of the file it has, and a cut of the picture across it is refused until the Music lane is locked or the clip removed*; *the file is missing, so its slice can only be shortened*). - [feat] **A video project can carry an edit — the ranges of its source that are kept — and the re-voice renders it** (porting vertical 6, phase E1: the model and the render, **no UI yet**). This is the first half of "build something like Camtasia": material you can cut. An **edit** is one thing, and it is the whole edit decision list: an ordered list of the `[start, end]` ranges of the project's one source that survive, in source seconds — `"edit": {"version": 1, "keep": [[0, 47.3], [52.3, 341.008]]}` on the project record — and **absent means keep everything**, so every project made before today renders exactly as it did and nothing is migrated. There is no clip object, no track object and no position field, because a split, a trim and a ripple delete are all changes to that one list: a split adds a boundary (two touching ranges where there was one, removing nothing), a trim moves a range's edge, and a ripple delete takes a sub-range out while the ranges either side stay where they are in source time. Position is derived, and that is what makes the second rule possible. **The transcript never moves.** The transcript Save decides that two entries are the same sentence when they have the same `start` and the same `end`, and carries a sentence's adjustments — its offset, mute, voice and speed — across only onto a match; cut ten seconds out of the picture the obvious way, by shifting every later sentence earlier, and every adjustment on every later sentence is dropped by construction, which is the corruption class phase 1 of the narration timeline was built to prevent, inverted. So the transcript stays in source seconds on disk, exactly as Whisper wrote it, and the edit is applied to it as a **projection** — computed by one function in `services/edit.py` at plan time and at render time, so the audition and the render cannot disagree about where a sentence lands. The rule at a cut is simple enough to say in one sentence: **a sentence is kept iff its start lies inside a kept range.** A sentence whose start was cut is left out exactly as a muted one is (the room it occupied goes to the sentence before it); a kept sentence's end is clamped to its range, so nothing is pinned past the output by the projection; every adjustment rides through untouched; and the offset in particular is left as stored, because the engine applies it itself — the pin is `to_timeline(start) + offset`, applied once. **The render is the same re-voice job**, started by the same `POST /api/projects/{pid}/revoice`, with one step in front of it when the edit removes anything: the kept ranges are re-encoded into a picture-only intermediate — `trim` / `setpts` / `concat` in one ffmpeg run at `libx264 -preset ultrafast`, the generate path's own settings, with no frame rate forced so the source's own cadence carries through — and the engine is then told *that* is the source, sees a transcript in a shorter recording and does what it always does; nothing in `_revoice_video` changed. It is a re-encode rather than a stream copy because a stream copy cannot start on the P-frames a cut lands on, and the source's keyframes are two seconds apart (measured), so snapping a cut to a keyframe could miss by more than a sentence; a whole re-encode of a five-minute 1080p source is about **16 s**, cheap enough to do on every render, as a job. Proven on the real 5m41s corpus: cut 47.3–52.3 s and the output is 336.008 s by the WAV arithmetic and 10079 frames / 335.967 s by the picture (the source's video stream is 41 ms shorter than its audio), opening on an I-frame at 0.000. An edit that removes nothing — a bare split — takes the untouched path, byte for byte, and no picture is cut. **This is an API ahead of its UI, and it is worth knowing before opening a project that has one.** Until phase E2 there is no gesture: nothing on the timeline cuts, and an edit is made by hand with `PUT /api/projects/{pid}/edit` (`{"keep": [[start, end], …]}`, replacing the whole list; `GET` reads it back with the source's and the output's length; `DELETE` goes back to keep-everything). Drawing the strip in timeline seconds, the range selection, ripple delete, the join marker, undo and the Render button are E2, and none of it exists. The routes follow the narration editor's rules exactly: the `GET` is allowed while a job holds the project, because it is a read; the `PUT` and the `DELETE` are a 409 while one does, and take the store's own lock around their read-modify-write, so a cut landing beside a per-sentence adjustment destroys neither (tested twenty times over on two threads); the body is strict (`extra="forbid"`; a boolean is not one second in) and a bad list is a 400 that names the range and stores nothing; a video that has not been transcribed is a 409, because the ranges are checked against the extracted audio's length and transcribing is what extracts it; and every write is audited as `project.edit` with the range count and the seconds removed — never the times, which would say where every cut is. Storing or clearing an edit also drops the memoised speaking rate, since that rate is measured over the sentences the render will speak and the edit decides which those are. Three refusals rather than silent wrong answers: an edit written by a different version of the app is refused rather than read as keep-everything (rendering the whole video while the user believes a cut is applied is the one thing this must never do quietly); an edit whose extracted audio has since gone is read back but neither applied nor ignored; and an edit that cuts every spoken sentence fails the job saying so. **One thing the Reviewer gate forced that a user would have felt:** `trim` is a filter and runs *after* the decode, so left to itself ffmpeg decodes every frame from the start of the file to the last kept range's end and throws most of them away — a 5 s keep at the tail of a two-hour recording would have been killed by a timeout sized to its 5 s of output. The picture step now seeks the input to the first kept range (`-ss` before `-i`, every trim offset by that origin; frame-exact — the decoded frames' `framemd5` is identical with and without the seek — and the tail keep on the corpus source fell from 3.9 s to 0.87 s), and its timeout follows **how far into the source the last kept range ends**, not the output length. It is also the one step of a re-voice that watches the cancel flag while ffmpeg runs, and Cancel kills the process rather than discarding its result when it is done; the job ends as cancelled with nothing rendered and nothing stamped. A render that applied an edit stamps `edit_rendered_at` beside `revoiced_at` (the output file keeps its name, so the cache-buster must move), and a whole-source run clears it, so the record never claims an edit the file does not carry. **One honest limitation, recorded rather than hidden:** `replace_video_audio` gained an explicit `video_duration` argument so the mux can be told an edited output's length — known before ffmpeg runs, as the sum of the kept ranges — instead of probing for it with the ffprobe the packaged app does not have. That model is a text editor and now says so (`extra="forbid"`: an adjustment key in its body is a 422, never a silent drop), and `set_transcript` carries the adjustments across itself — by index, and only onto a segment that is still the same sentence's **window**, because the length of the list is not identity: a save that deletes one sentence and adds another keeps the count, and an index-only rule would slide every adjustment down a row onto a sentence it was never meant for. What cannot be carried is counted, returned as `timing_adjustments_dropped` and shown, rather than lost quietly. What an offset can promise is written on the card, because a pin is a **floor, not a position**: a sentence can always be pushed later, but pulling it earlier only moves it as far as the sentence before it finishes speaking. The re-voice honours all four — the pin moves by the offset (and by the *next* sentence's offset too, or the tempo squeeze fires against a window nothing will use), a muted sentence is dropped before the window maths so the sentence before it inherits its room and the last unmuted one still runs to the end of the video, an explicit speed is used verbatim and skips both the per-sentence estimate and the post-synthesis squeeze, and a per-sentence voice falls back cleanly when it belongs to the other provider. Muting every sentence is refused with a reason (400) rather than failing inside the job as a bare "Re-voice failed", and the re-voice card says plainly that the adjustments do not apply to a **translated** re-voice: translation joins the whole transcript into one block before it runs, so the engine re-cuts it and spreads the sentences across the video by length, and no sentence survives for them to belong to. `services/narration.py`, `api/routers/narration.py`, `api/schemas.py` (`SegmentOverride` — five fields, strict so a boolean is not coerced to 1.0), `services/processing.py`, `services/revoice.py`, `frontend/src/pages/ProjectDetail.tsx` and `lib/format.ts` (a separate `timecode()` in `m:ss.mmm`; `duration()` rounds to the second and is left alone).
